# Exercise 2: Handle Difficult Questions

> Practice responding to challenging audience questions.

---

## Scenario

You're presenting a modernization proposal. The audience asks difficult questions.

---

## Practice Scenarios

### Question 1: "This sounds expensive."

**Bad response:**
> "Well, it's not that expensive considering..."

**Better response:**
> "I understand budget is a concern. Let me address that. Our proposal includes a phased approach that allows you to spread investment over time. Phase 1 can begin at $X, delivering immediate value. Would it help if I walked through the phased options?"

---

### Question 2: "We've been told ColdFusion is dying."

**Bad response:**
> "ColdFusion isn't dying, that's just perception..."

**Better response:**
> "That's a common concern. The reality is more nuanced. Adobe continues to release ColdFusion, and Lucee provides an active open-source path. What we're seeing is not a death spiral but a consolidation—companies are choosing to either invest in their CF systems or migrate. Both paths are viable. The question is which fits your situation best."

---

### Question 3: "Can you guarantee the timeline?"

**Bad response:**
> "Yes, we'll hit the deadline."

**Better response:**
> "I can't guarantee an exact date without completing discovery, but I can tell you what affects timeline: scope clarity, feedback speed, and testing availability. If those are addressed, our estimates are typically within 15% of actual. What I can commit to is weekly updates and proactive communication if anything changes."
