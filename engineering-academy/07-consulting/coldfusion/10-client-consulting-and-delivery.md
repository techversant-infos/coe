# 10. Client Consulting and Delivery

**Status:** Draft · **Owner:** CoE Team · **Last updated:** 2026-11-05 · **Next review:** 2027-02-05

Technical skill wins the work; consulting skill keeps it. A senior consultant runs discovery, estimates honestly,
writes proposals, presents to decision makers and manages the engagement through to handover.
Back to the [roadmap](./README.md).

## Discovery workshops

Discovery turns a vague request into a clear problem, goals and constraints.

**What to learn:**

- Planning a workshop: the people, the agenda and the questions
- Asking about the business first, then the application
- Separating facts from guesses, and confirming what you heard in writing

**Resources:**

- [Discovery Workshop Questionnaire](./deliverables/discovery-workshop-questionnaire.md)
- [Client Communication Guide: during discovery sessions](./deliverables/client-communication-guide.md#during-discovery-sessions)

**Level:** Practitioner · Core

## Estimation

An estimate is a range with stated assumptions, not a single number.

**What to learn:**

- Breaking the work into packages with a work breakdown structure
- Three-point estimates: optimistic, likely and pessimistic
- Adding risk buffers and writing every assumption down

**Resources:**

- [Estimation templates](./deliverables/estimation-templates/README.md)
- [Estimation prompts](../../../ai/prompts/section-04-estimation.md)

**Level:** Practitioner · Core

## Proposals and statements of work

The proposal sells the approach; the statement of work (SOW) fixes the scope, deliverables and terms.

**What to learn:**

- Writing the problem, approach, deliverables and assumptions in the client's language
- Defining what is out of scope
- Using the standard engagement types: assessment, migration and ongoing retainer

**Resources:**

- [Proposal scopes](./deliverables/proposal-boilerplates/proposal-scopes.md)
- [SOW template](./deliverables/sow-template.md)

**Level:** Advanced · Core

## Client communication

Clear, early and honest communication prevents most client problems.

**What to learn:**

- Explaining technical findings in terms of business impact
- Saying "I don't know yet" and giving a date for the answer
- Raising risks, delays and scope changes as soon as you see them

**Resources:** [Client Communication Guide](./deliverables/client-communication-guide.md)

**Level:** Practitioner · Core

## Presenting to decision makers

Executives decide on budget and risk, not on code.

**What to learn:**

- Starting with the recommendation and the business reason
- Showing options with their cost, risk and timeline
- Handling questions, including ones you cannot answer on the spot

**Resources:** [Client Communication Guide: explaining technical findings](./deliverables/client-communication-guide.md#explaining-technical-findings)

**Level:** Advanced · Core

## Running the engagement

A senior consultant owns the delivery: plan, status, quality, risks and changes.

**What to learn:**

- The engagement lifecycle: qualification, discovery, proposal, delivery and quality review
- Weekly status, a risk register and an assumptions log
- Change requests: assessing, pricing and agreeing them before doing the work
- Peer review and escalation inside Techversant

**Resources:**

- [Consulting Operating Model](./deliverables/consulting-operating-model.md)
- [Engagement Checklist](./deliverables/engagement-checklist.md)

**Level:** Advanced · Core

## Handover and knowledge transfer

An engagement is finished when the client's team can run, change and roll back the system without you.

**What to learn:**

- Planning the handover from the start, not in the last week
- A handover pack: system overview, runbooks, access list, open risks and known issues
- Shadowing in both directions: the client's team watches you, then you watch them do the work

**Resources:**

- [Engagement Checklist](./deliverables/engagement-checklist.md)
- [Communication: handoff note template](../../00-engineering-foundations/04-communication.md#handoff-note)

**Level:** Advanced · Core

## Leading and mentoring

Lead consultants grow the next ones.

**What to learn:**

- Letting junior consultants shadow, then co-deliver, then lead with your review
- Reviewing their reports and estimates with specific feedback

**Resources:** learn on engagements, with the practice lead.

**Level:** Expert · Optional

## Project: Run a Mock Engagement

**Brief:** A senior colleague plays one of the [practice clients](./reference/practice-clients.md). Run the engagement
from first meeting to handover, using your work from the earlier projects.

**Size:** 2 weeks part-time

**Verification**, checked by the colleague playing the client and your lead:

- [ ] A discovery workshop with the questionnaire, and notes confirmed in writing by the "client"
- [ ] An estimate as a range, built from a work breakdown structure, with every assumption listed
- [ ] A proposal and SOW with deliverables, out-of-scope items and terms, agreed by the "client"
- [ ] A 20-minute presentation to the "decision makers" with a clear recommendation and options
- [ ] Weekly status updates, a risk register, and one change request handled through the process
- [ ] A handover pack that someone else uses to run and support the system without help
