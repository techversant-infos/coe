# Exercise 4: Difficult Conversation Roleplay

> Practice handling a frustrated or challenging client conversation.

---

## Setup

Pair up with a colleague:
- One plays the frustrated client
- One plays the consultant

---

## Scenario: The Frustrated Client

**Setting:** Weekly status call, week 4 of a CF migration project

**Client says:**
> "I've been hearing from my team that things are taking longer than expected. We've paid for a 6-week project and we're on week 4 and you haven't even started on the database migration yet. What exactly is happening?"

**Client's body language:** Arms crossed, leaning back, looks at watch

---

## Your Response Framework

### Step 1: Acknowledge (Don't Defend)

**Start with:**
> "I understand your frustration, and I appreciate you raising this directly. Let me address your concerns."

### Step 2: Explain Context

**Present the facts:**
> "Here's where we are: [explain what was completed]. [Explain why database hasn't started]."

### Step 3: Show the Impact

**Connect to their goals:**
> "The reason we haven't started database yet is [reason]. This was actually the right call because [benefit]."

### Step 4: Present Options

**Offer choices:**
> "We have a few options:
> 1. [Option A with trade-offs]
> 2. [Option B with trade-offs]
> 3. [Option C with trade-offs]"

### Step 5: Get Commitment

**Close with action:**
> "What would you like us to do? Once you decide, we'll update the schedule accordingly."

---

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Why It's Bad | Better Approach |
|---------|--------------|----------------|
| "It's not our fault" | Defensive | Acknowledge first |
| "Your team should have..." | Blaming | Focus on solutions |
| "Let me explain..." | Defending | Lead with understanding |
| Ignoring body language | Misses cues | Match their energy |
